T-XMER: Trustworthy Multimodal Emotion Recognition

Evaluating Reliability and Explanation Faithfulness under Noisy, Missing, and Conflicting Modalities

Multimodal Emotion Recognition (MER) models frequently experience performance collapse and explanation degradation when real-world inputs suffer from sensory noise, dropped modalities, or contradictory cross-modal signals. T-XMER develops evaluation protocols and methodological frameworks to measure model robustness and verify whether explanation mechanisms remain faithful when modalities are impaired.

Reliability Analysis
Assessing performance resilience against corrupted and incomplete sensory data streams.
Explanation Faithfulness
Verifying that feature attributions mirror actual model reasoning without hallucination.
Cross-Modal Conflict
Disentangling contradictory signals across audio, visual, and textual modalities.
Keywords: Multimodal Learning Explainable AI Robust AI Trustworthy AI Emotion Recognition

Research Scope

T-XMER: Detailed Research Overview

Trustworthy Multimodal Emotion Recognition

Core Objective: Investigate the resilience and interpretability of multimodal emotion recognition models when deployed in non-ideal environments characterized by sensor degradation, missing sensory streams, and contradictory cross-modal signals.

Research Challenges Addressed:

  • Noisy Modalities: Auditory background noise, visual occlusion, and textual ambiguity.
  • Missing Modalities: Sensor failure or communication latency leading to incomplete input sets.
  • Conflicting Modalities: Discrepancies between facial expressions, vocal tone, and transcribed text.
  • Explanation Faithfulness: Ensuring that attribution methods faithfully represent the true decision dynamics of the fusion architecture.

Methodological Direction: Developing robust multimodal representation learning and diagnostic benchmarking frameworks that evaluate both empirical accuracy and explainability under stress conditions.
